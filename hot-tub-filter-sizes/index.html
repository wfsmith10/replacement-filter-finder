Hot tub filter sizes: spa cartridges by measured diameter and length

Edited by William Smith · researched with AI assistance · Last updated 2026-11-28 · How we make pages

Short answer: measure the outside diameter and the length from one end cap to the other, then find that size in the tables below. Then match both ends too. In Pleatco's 2025–26 catalog, several spa cartridges share the same diameter and length but have a different thread or a different filter area. Pleatco's part number often tells you the thread: a P3 ending means a 1-1/2″ SAE thread, P4 means a 1-1/2″ male pipe thread (MPT) and F2M means a 2″ male pipe thread. Also expect the brands to print slightly different sizes for the same cartridge. For 24 of the 52 spa cartridges below that have a Unicel equivalent, Unicel's printed diameter or length differs from Pleatco's by more than the 1/16″ that Pleatco tells you to allow.

People ask this. One example we found through web search (the title is taken from the thread URL; we did not open it): "how do I know which filter replacement to buy on Amazon" on r/hottub. We have not measured, tested or used any cartridge ourselves. Every size, end type, area and part number on this page is copied from the Pleatco and Unicel catalogs listed under Sources. Where the two catalogs disagree, we show both and do not pick one.

Step 1: measure the way the catalogs measure

First look for a part number on both end caps. If you find one, look for it in the tables below: Pleatco numbers are in the first column, and the Unicel and Filbur numbers Pleatco lists are in the last two. (For pool cartridges, use our Pleatco, Unicel & Filbur cross-reference.) If there is no number, our guide to measuring an unlabeled cartridge compares the instructions from Pleatco, Unicel and Filbur. In short:

  • Diameter: the outside diameter of the cartridge.
  • Length: end cap to end cap. Pleatco's catalog says "Only measure from end-cap to end cap! Do not include handles or thread" (Pleatco 2023 catalog).
  • Both ends: open (measure the hole), closed, handle, or a threaded or slip fitting. Many of the spa cartridges in the tables below have a threaded, keyed or slip-fit bottom end.
  • Allow for small differences: Pleatco says "Measurements may vary. Always allow a variance of (+/- 1/16 inch)" (Pleatco 2023 catalog).

Step 2: read the thread code in a Pleatco part number

Pleatco's 2023 catalog has a key of "Pleatco thread codes". The code is part of many spa cartridge numbers, so it tells you the fitting even before you look at the size. This is Pleatco's own list, copied as printed:

In the 2025–26 measurement tables, the listed bottom end matches this key for 28 of the 29 spa cartridges whose number carries one of these codes: every P3 cartridge is listed as "1 1/2″ SAE", every P4 as "1 1/2″ MPT", every F2M as "2″ MPT" and every F2S as "2″ SAE". The one exception is PSG31-XP4: it carries the XP4 code, but its bottom end is listed as "1 1/2″ MPT", not 1.90″ keyed. So use the code as a first clue and still check the listed ends. Unicel writes the thread in its end-cap description instead, for example "1-1/2″ male thread/MPT" or "injection molded 1-1/2″ SAE thread" (Unicel 2026 Replacement Guide).

For a thread difference, compare the old cartridge's threaded end with the fitting in the filter housing, or look for the code in the part number. For an area difference, the catalog's sq ft figure is the tie-breaker; the Unicel guide also prints a pleat count for each cartridge. Pleatco lists these as separate part numbers, so we treat them as different cartridges. We cannot tell you whether a cartridge with the other thread would fit your housing.

Step 4: expect Pleatco and Unicel to print different sizes

For 52 of the 68 spa cartridges, Pleatco's 2025–26 catalog lists a Unicel equivalent. We looked up each of those Unicel numbers in the Unicel 2026 Replacement Guide and compared the printed sizes:

  • 14 of 52 have the same diameter and the same length in both catalogs.
  • 14 more differ by 1/16″ or less, within the variance Pleatco tells you to allow. A common pattern: Pleatco prints 5″ where Unicel prints 4-15/16″ (PRB25-IN and C-4326, for example).
  • 24 of 52 differ by more than 1/16″ in diameter or length. The largest gaps are in length: PWW35L is 8-1/2″ in Pleatco's table and its listed Unicel equivalent 4CH-935 is 9-1/4″ (3/4″ longer); PTL47W-P4 and 6CH-47 differ by 5/8″ (8-1/2″ vs 9-1/8″), PWW50P3 and 6CH-940 by 5/8″ (7-5/8″ vs 8-1/4″), and PJW60TL-F2S and 6CH-960 by 5/8″ (14-7/8″ vs 15-1/2″).
  • 22 of 52 print a different filter area. For example, Pleatco lists PBF35 at 35 sq ft and its Unicel equivalent 8CH-950 at 50 sq ft, and PCAL60-F2M at 60 sq ft against 75 sq ft for 5CH-752.

We cannot say which catalog is right, and an equivalence listing is Pleatco's claim, not ours. What this means in practice: if your measurement is a little off from one brand's size, check the other brand's printed size before you decide your cartridge is not listed. For the Waterway Teleweir cartridges, the two catalogs also describe the threaded end differently. Pleatco lists the bottom of PWW25L, PWW35L and PWW50L as 1-1/2″ female SAE. Unicel lists 4CH-935 and 4CH-949 as "2″ female SAE thread" with the note "For use on 1-1/2″ male SAE stand pipe." So the same Waterway cartridge can appear as "1-1/2″" in one catalog and "2″" in the other. Unicel's note suggests the 1-1/2″ figure is the size of the standpipe the cartridge threads onto.

How to use these tables

  1. Find your diameter in the right table, then your length. Allow about 1/16″ either way, and look at the Unicel size too.
  2. Match the top end and the bottom end. If the bottom is threaded, match the thread type (SAE, MPT, ACME, keyed or slip) and size, not just "threaded".
  3. If two rows still match, compare the filter area (sq ft) printed on your old cartridge or in your spa's manual.
  4. Take the part number from the row and check it on the maker's own site or in its catalog before you order.

Not listed here: hot tub cartridges that appear only in the Unicel or Filbur catalogs, and Pleatco cartridges that the 2025–26 catalog labels "Pool" rather than "Spa". The Unicel 2026 guide lists other spa cartridges that are not on this page.
