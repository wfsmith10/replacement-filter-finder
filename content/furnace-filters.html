Most forced-air systems take a disposable filter in a slot or return-air grille. Getting a replacement that fits comes down to three things printed on most filter frames: the size, the depth, and the rating.

The basics

  • Nominal vs. actual size. The size printed in large type (for example "16x25x1") is a rounded nominal size. The actual frame is usually slightly smaller and is often printed in smaller type on the frame. Measure your current filter or the slot and compare actual dimensions. See nominal vs. actual sizes from four brands.
  • Depth. Filters come in different thicknesses. A deeper filter will not fit a slot built for a thinner one, so match the depth your system was designed for.
  • MERV. MERV (Minimum Efficiency Reporting Value) is a rating scale defined in ASHRAE Standard 52.2 for how well a filter captures particles of different sizes in a lab test. Your equipment maker's manual is the place to check which ratings and depths your system supports.
