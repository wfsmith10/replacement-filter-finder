Short answer: buy the nominal size, the rounded number printed in large type on your old filter (for example 16x25x1). The filter itself is a little smaller. Published actual sizes for a 16x25x1 range from 15.5 x 24.5 x 0.75 inches (Filterbuy, AAF Flanders) to 15.75 x 24.75 x 0.75 inches (Honeywell), with Filtrete in between at 15.69 x 24.69 inches. All of them are sold as "16x25x1". If the old filter is unreadable, measure it and round each number up to the next whole inch, unless your slot is a true odd size (see step 3 below).

What the chart shows

  • Every brand is smaller than the label, by different amounts. Filterbuy and AAF Flanders run ½ inch under on width and height. Honeywell runs ¼ inch under. Filtrete runs about 0.31 inch under (15.69 for 16).
  • The gap between brands is up to ¼ inch per side. A Honeywell 16x25x1 (15.75 x 24.75) is ¼ inch wider and ¼ inch taller than a Filterbuy or AAF Flanders 16x25x1 (15.5 x 24.5). That is why a filter that fit snugly can feel loose, or tight, after you switch brands.
  • "1-inch" filters are about ¾ inch thick. The published depths are 0.75 inch (Filterbuy, Honeywell, AAF Flanders) and 0.78 or 0.81 inch (Filtrete, depending on the product).
  • Face area is a few percent below the nominal 400 square inches for a 16x25. Our arithmetic from the published sizes: 15.5 x 24.5 = 379.75 sq in (about 95% of 400); 15.69 x 24.69 = 387.39 sq in (about 97%); 15.75 x 24.75 = 389.81 sq in (about 97.5%). If you are doing the airflow math on our MERV 13 in a 1-inch slot page, the actual area is the more accurate number.

Many 4- and 5-inch filters are sold by the cabinet they fit rather than by a generic size. Which number to buy: a 6-step method

  1. Read the old filter's frame. AirFilters.com says most filters print "the nominal size in large print and the actual size in small print." The large number (for example 16x25x1) is the one to order. Note the small actual size too.
  2. If the frame is unreadable, measure the old filter (width, height, depth) and round each number up to the nearest inch. That is Nordic Pure's method for finding the nominal size. Example: 15¾ x 24¾ x ¾ becomes 16x25x1.
  3. Don't round a true odd size. Some filters are "actual size": AirFilters.com's example is a frame marked 16.5x21x1 that measures exactly 16.5 x 21. Filterbuy says that if your slot "lands on a true fraction rather than close to a whole inch, that is a custom slot, so do not round."
  4. If there is no old filter, measure the slot (the inside of the opening) and, per Nordic Pure, subtract ¼ to ½ inch for clearance. Then pick a filter whose published actual size is slightly smaller than the opening.
  5. Check the actual size before you switch brands. Compare the new brand's published actual size (the chart above, or the product listing) with your old filter. Nordic Pure: "Switching brands? Always verify actual dimensions."
  6. Check the fit when you install it. The U.S. EPA's guide says: "Make sure the filter fits snugly so air does not leak around the filter and you do not have to bend or crush the filter to make it fit." Match the third number (depth) too; the EPA guide notes filters commonly come in thicknesses of 1 to 5 inches.

Where sources disagree, or are wrong

  • "About ½ inch under" vs. "about ¼ inch under". AirFilters.com's FAQ says nominal-size filters "generally measure ½” under" the printed size, but the same FAQ also says a 16x25x1 "usually measures about 15.75x24.75x0.75", which is ¼ inch under. Both kinds exist (see the chart). Nordic Pure gives a range: "usually 0.25″–0.5″ smaller."
  • No single standard. Filterbuy writes that its half-inch convention "is not a published standard and it is not universal." None of the other sources we read cites an industry standard for actual sizes either.
  • Retail listings can repeat the nominal size. The Home Depot Canada listing for a Filtrete 16x25x1 MERV 11 four-pack shows "Actual Size" as 16.0 x 25.0 x 1.0, the same as the nominal size, while the single-filter Filtrete listings on the same site show 15.69 x 24.69. If an "actual size" exactly equals the nominal size on a standard filter, treat it as unconfirmed and check the frame or another listing.
  • Same brand, different depth. Filtrete's published depth is 0.78 inch on its 16x25x1 MERV 14 listing and 0.81 inch on its 20x20x1 and 20x25x1 MERV 11 listings. A few hundredths rarely matter in a 1-inch slot, but it shows that "actual size" can vary even within a brand.

What we could not confirm

  • We did not find a readable Filtrete or Nordic Pure size chart on their own sites (their product pages fill in the size by script). Filtrete numbers come from a large retailer's listings; Nordic Pure's 15.5 x 24.5 x 0.75 is the example in its own measuring guide, not a product listing.
  • Honeywell's figures are for its MERV 11 1-inch line on the Honeywell Store. Other Honeywell lines may be cut differently.
  • Makers change products. Check the listing you are actually buying from.
